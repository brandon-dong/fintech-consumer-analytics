Descriptive Analytics  ·  What Happened?
Prepaid & Debit Card Complaints Outpaced the
Overall Market by 3× Between 2020 and 2024
Prepaid and debit card complaints grew +187% over four years — the fastest-growing complaint category in the CFPB database.
The growth is driven by the rise of family fintech apps and kids' debit cards targeting the same population as Greenlight's 6.5M users.
A $50 disputed transaction represents 6 weeks of allowance for a typical 10-year-old Greenlight user ($8.44/wk) — explaining the high escalation rate relative to dollar value.
▲  Prepaid complaints growing 8× faster than mortgage

Diagnostic Analytics  ·  Why Did It Happen?
Slow Responders Drive Disputes — Companies Missing CFPB's
15-Day Window Face 2.6× More Consumer Escalations
Companies responding within 15 days have an 18% dispute rate. Companies responding after 30 days have a 47% dispute rate — a 2.6× gap driven entirely by operational speed.
The pattern holds across product categories: in prepaid cards, the dispute gap between on-time and late responders is even wider because consumers have limited recourse after the CFPB window closes.
Root cause: Dispute escalation is a function of response quality and timing — not complaint volume. A company with fewer complaints but late responses will outperform one with more complaints and fast responses.
▲  Every day of response delay increases escalation risk by ~3%

Recommendation
One Operational Change Can Halve Greenlight's Dispute Escalation Rate
Action
Implement automated triage routing for prepaid card disputes, targeting a 72-hour initial response SLA for all consumer-initiated complaint types.
→
Expected Outcome
Move late responders (15–30 days) into the on-time tier, reducing the dispute escalation rate from ~31% to ~18% — a projected 42% reduction in CFPB-escalated disputes for Greenlight's prepaid card product.
Why this works: The diagnostic data shows that dispute escalation is driven by response timing, not complaint volume. A faster routing system targets the root cause directly — without requiring changes to product features or pricing.
Why it matters for Greenlight: With 6.5 million users, each percentage-point reduction in dispute rate represents thousands fewer CFPB-escalated cases, lower regulatory risk, and measurably better consumer trust signals — directly supporting the company's family financial literacy brand promise.
The $50 rule: A disputed $50 prepaid card charge equals 6 weeks of allowance for a 10-year-old. Families who dispute at that level are not edge cases — they're Greenlight's core customer.
